## Game rules and balance assessment

The game exceeds a decorative interaction: charge/release/coast is the dominant action, with heat, lane choice, collision resilience and unbanked delivery chains as coupled systems. Breakable gates give dash a real consequence; solid gates require avoidance. The second phase adds lane changes before gate lock and the third adds optional higher-value branches. These are decision changes rather than only faster spawning.

The rules specify initialization, thresholds, token identity, banking eligibility, unique zone spending, collision penalties, ordered updates, finish cutoff, scoring, success/failure and complete replay reset. Four safe banking intervals offer one missed opportunity while success requires three banks and nine secured tokens. The proposed cooling allowance is arithmetically consistent: cooling from 1 to .4 at .25/s requires 2.4 simulation seconds. At the declared threshold, three banks of three tokens meet the success requirement. A traversable route with at least four reachable tokens per interval is a concrete generation constraint to prove, not an achieved balance claim.

Pause freezes active dash duration/offset and simulation time while canceling unlaunched charge and held input. This avoids turning a pause into an accidental release or moving hazards during hidden time. Terminal state and run-ID commits prevent replay/duplicate result corruption.

The first balance proof must test the practical value of longer charging against minimum-charge dashes, actual forward-offset/collision geometry, and readable telegraphs at the maximum allowed closing speed. These are already within the packet's tuning and fairness proof scope. A numerical rules harness alone cannot demonstrate that heat, greed and lane choice feel useful. No new preproduction gate is needed.
