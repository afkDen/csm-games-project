# Spatial Depth Contract — Do Not Let the Experience Feel Flat

The project should feel **inhabitable, layered, and physically responsive**, even though much of its primary artwork begins as still imagery.

## Core rule

A major cinematic scene should not default to `flat image + text + particles` when a stronger spatial treatment is feasible.

Depth can come from 2.5D, 3D, compositing, optical distortion, camera behavior, atmosphere, occlusion, typography, or combinations of them. Full 3D is not mandatory; convincing spatial relationships are.

## Meaningful depth relationships

Where the still supports it, identify a small number of purposeful layers such as:

- foreground occluder / eyewear / hand / graphic element;
- primary character plane;
- middle environment;
- distant environment;
- atmosphere / light / particles;
- typography or lens layer that can move through depth.

Prefer 3–7 strong relationships over dozens of fragile micro-cutouts.

## Camera must cross relationships

Parallax should not be a decorative ±10 px drift alone. At important beats, camera or layer motion should produce a readable change in occlusion, perspective, scale relationship, focus, or spatial ownership.

At least one major beat in each route should make the visitor feel that they moved **into, through, around, or behind** part of the still/world.

## Foreground is valuable

Use foreground elements intentionally to create occlusion, reveal, scale, and camera passage. Foreground may come from the source still, derived masks, sourced supporting assets, procedural shapes, 3D props, atmosphere, or typography.

## Typography can occupy space

Type may pass behind the character, intersect masks, travel on a separate depth plane, assemble with scroll, or become part of the lens/portal composition. Do not confine every title to a flat DOM overlay above the visual world.

## Optical depth

The eyewear/lens concept can contribute depth through:

- refraction / displacement;
- reflection and glare;
- focus shifts;
- chromatic separation used sparingly;
- depth-sensitive blur;
- masks that reveal another spatial layer;
- lens surfaces that become a true compositing boundary.

## Environmental richness

When a scene feels sparse, do not automatically add generic particles. Diagnose the missing dimension:

- foreground framing?
- middle-distance structure?
- atmospheric perspective?
- lighting variation?
- environmental motion?
- scale reference?
- surface/material information?
- audio depth?

Then use `asset-scout` or procedural work to fill the actual gap.

## Still integrity

Spatialization must preserve illustration quality. Faces, hands, eyewear, and key silhouette regions should not warp merely to create movement. Depth treatment should reveal the still, not damage it.

## Flatness review

A reviewer should flag a major route state when most of these are true:

- the character art behaves as one undifferentiated plane;
- background and foreground have no meaningful relative motion;
- camera movement is effectively a uniform scale tween;
- text always sits in a separate flat overlay;
- atmosphere does not convey depth;
- pointer/scroll does not alter spatial relationships;
- the lens is decorative rather than spatial;
- stopping at intermediate scroll states reveals no composed depth.

A single flat beat can be intentional contrast. A flat route cannot be the default outcome.
