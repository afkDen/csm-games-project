# Creative Direction Contract — Still-Led Pastel Motion World

This is a **locked creative contract**. It resolves the visual hierarchy for the whole project.

## North star

The website should feel like the supplied character stills have been **art-directed into a bright, pastel, spatial, reactive motion experience**.

Three truths operate in this order:

1. **The stills define visual truth.** Their composition, focal hierarchy, hue relationships, lighting character, texture, crop, and emotional atmosphere are the primary reference.
2. **The character defines behavioral truth.** Personality from the source material should be translated into pacing, camera behavior, interaction, typography, audio, and scene psychology.
3. **The project defines tonal reinterpretation.** The overall site is colorful, pastel-forward, playful, polished, airy, and premium rather than defaulting to a dark/gritty anime-promo mood.

A route fails if it is recognizably the character only because it uses that character's image or canonical color.

## Still-first visual authority

Before designing a route, create a still-aesthetic profile from the approved stills:

- dominant and secondary hue families;
- highlight/accent colors;
- shadow temperature;
- saturation/chroma behavior;
- contrast/value structure;
- focal subject and crop logic;
- negative-space pattern;
- foreground/midground/background relationships;
- lighting softness/hardness and direction;
- texture/grain/print/lens qualities;
- regions that must remain visually stable;
- motifs worth carrying into type, shapes, VFX, and UI.

Do **not** start from a generic character palette and tint the scene later.

## Pastel reinterpretation

Pastel does not mean weak, childish, or uniformly desaturated. It means the project-wide base environment favors luminous, softened, color-rich values and playful polish.

When the source still is darker, preserve its hue logic, dramatic relationships, and focal accents while allowing the surrounding world to lift into:

- cream / pearl / warm white rather than default black;
- soft pink, peach, coral, butter yellow;
- sky blue, powder blue, lilac, lavender;
- mint, aqua, pale teal;
- translucent glass and optical color mixing;
- soft bloom, haze, grain, diffusion, and colored shadow.

Dark values are allowed as **contrast, punctuation, tension, or a route-specific beat**. They should not become the automatic global background language.

Do not destructively recolor the character still merely to force it pastel. Build the pastel system around the still's actual visual logic.

## Character-authentic, not mood-literal

Preserve recognizable character presence through behavior rather than copying the source work's darkest tonal defaults.

Character fidelity should shape:

- pace and rhythm;
- symmetry / asymmetry;
- camera confidence or instability;
- how much the world yields to the pointer;
- scroll tension and release;
- typography attitude;
- transition temperament;
- signature interaction;
- audio texture and use of silence;
- minigame fantasy and feedback.

Use `docs/CHARACTER_FIDELITY_MATRIX.md` as a starting hypothesis, then refine from the supplied stills and source-material research without inventing unsupported canon claims.

## Project-wide visual language

Favor:

- expressive editorial composition;
- layered still-image worlds;
- translucent / refractive lens surfaces;
- playful graphic shapes used with purpose;
- luminous gradients and optical color mixing;
- tactile micro-motion;
- large type that participates in depth and choreography;
- controlled bloom, softness, halation, grain, and texture;
- moments that remain strong when paused as portfolio stills.

Avoid as defaults:

- black/red/grunge as shorthand for Chainsaw Man;
- generic cyberpunk/neon styling;
- horror-first presentation across the whole site;
- generic glassmorphism card systems;
- SaaS section rhythm;
- decorative 3D objects with no character or compositional role;
- a dark WebGL scene with bright UI layered on top;
- flattening supplied stills into background wallpaper.

## Screenshot rule

Every major entry, midpoint, peak, interactive hold, portal state, game handoff, and result state should be intentionally composed enough to work as a still frame.

The screenshot rule does **not** mean motion should be timid. The experience should look excellent both while moving and when frozen.

## Review gate

A route cannot pass because it is technically impressive alone. Reviewers must answer:

1. Does the scene visibly derive from its supplied stills rather than a generic anime aesthetic?
2. Does the character feel behaviorally recognizable even with the character image hidden?
3. Is the overall route still part of the bright/pastel/colorful project family?
4. Do dark moments function as intentional contrast rather than the default mood?
5. Does typography, UI, VFX, sound, and motion support the still instead of competing with it?
6. Are the strongest frames composed rather than merely busy?

If any answer is materially no, the route requires revision.
