# Product Truth

## What this is

An unofficial portfolio interaction study: a playable, motion-led web exhibition inspired by the visual idea of characters holding eyewear frames and being seen through the lenses.

## Audience

- creative developers
- frontend / WebGL engineers
- motion designers
- recruiters / collaborators evaluating interactive portfolio work
- anime / design enthusiasts who encounter the piece organically

## Core user promise

Entering a pair of glasses should feel like crossing into a character's authored world and **driving that world through motion**, not opening another content section.

## Primary experience

A visitor explores a bright pastel lens gallery, enters a character route whose visual language is derived from supplied stills, continuously scrolls through a layered spatial composition, triggers a character-specific signature interaction and cinematic peak, crosses the lens into a playable chibi minigame, earns a visible result/unlock, and returns through the lens to the hub.

## Creative truth

- supplied stills are the primary visual authority;
- character personality is translated into behavior/motion/composition rather than only palette;
- the overall website is colorful, pastel-forward, playful, polished, and premium rather than dark-by-default;
- scroll is a timeline and interaction input, not merely vertical navigation;
- the experience should feel layered and inhabitable rather than flat;
- supporting assets may be actively discovered/generated/derived/procedurally built when a defined scene needs them.

## Not the product

- ecommerce
- a JINS redesign proposal presented as official
- an anime streaming site
- a visual novel
- a static anime fan page
- a dark/gritty promo template
- five unrelated game demos
- five reskinned scroll pages

## Content truth

The project owner intends to supply campaign/anime stills, including character images holding glasses. Chibi art may be owner-supplied, generated under a separately approved workflow, or built as original simplified representations. Do not assume a specific source until provenance is recorded.

## Success criteria

The piece should be memorable for the lens portal, still-led pastel art direction, continuous scroll choreography, spatial depth, character-authentic interaction, world-to-world contrast, minigame quality, and coherent audio/visual feedback.

A route can fail despite functioning correctly if it feels flat, section-based, generic, dark-by-default, weakly tied to its stills, or behaviorally interchangeable with another character.
